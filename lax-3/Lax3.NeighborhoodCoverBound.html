Theorem

Every graph has, for every radius r, an r-neighborhood cover of radius 2r whose degree is at most the weak 2r-coloring number of the graph.

This is Theorem 6.2 of Grohe–Kreutzer–Siebertz (via their Lemma 6.9): from a vertex ordering witnessing the weak coloring number, take as the cluster of v the set of vertices from which v is weakly 2r-reachable. On a nowhere dense class this composes with Lax12's subpolynomial weak coloring numbers to covers of degree c · n^ε for every ε > 0 — the form the model-checking recursion consumes, on every arena, since the weak coloring bound of Lax12 is uniform over subgraphs of members.

22# Formalization notes
23
24The statement is per-graph and class-free, with the degree bound
25`wcol G (2r)` — Lax12's `wcol`, not restated. This is the strongest
26form the construction gives: the covering and radius properties hold
27for the clusters of an arbitrary ordering, and only the degree needs
28the ordering to be a good one, so the theorem composes with any wcol
29bound a consumer owns, not only the nowhere dense one. The n^ε class
30form is a corollary consumers derive by picking the ordering from
31`hasSubpolynomialWcol_of_nowhereDense`; it is deliberately not a
32second axiom.
33
34The discharge constructs the cluster of `u` as
35`{w | u ∈ wreach G π (2r) w}` for an optimal ordering `π` and reads
36the degree bound off `(wreach G π (2r) v).ncard ≤ wcol G (2r)`
37directly; covering and radius are elementary walk arguments. The
38*computation* of such a cover on the word RAM — including computing a
39good-enough ordering — is the algorithmic content of a later phase
40(the augmentation-density work of the campaign plan) and is not part
41of this statement.
