      Theorem

      If the Post correspondence problem were decidable, then the acceptance problem for Turing machines would be decidable as well (Sipser, Theorem 5.15, as a many-one reduction). From a machine MMM and an input www one constructs, by Sipser's seven kinds of dominos, an instance whose matches are exactly the accepting computation histories of MMM on www — the strings # C1 # C2 #⋯# Ck #\#\,C_1\,\#\,C_2\,\#\cdots\#\,C_k\,\##C1​#C2​#⋯#Ck​# of consecutive configurations from q0wq_0 wq0​w to a configuration in the accept state, each obtained from the previous one by a step of the machine; the passage through the modified problem, in which a match must start with the first domino, is the ⋆\star⋆ trick. The construction is effective, indeed primitive recursive, which is the part of the argument that the textbook takes for granted.

      Formalization notes

      Both problems are stated over the alphabet NℕN with mathlib's ComputablePredComputablePredComputablePred: Sipser's instance is built over the alphabet SymSymSym of configurations and histories, and the proof transports it to NℕN along an injective encoding of SymSymSym, which preserves and reflects the existence of a match.
