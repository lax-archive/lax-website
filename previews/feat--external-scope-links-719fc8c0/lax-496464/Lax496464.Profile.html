      Natural Language Statement

      Definition

      The algorithm behind the second half of the third theorem. It sweeps the start times in order, and at the start time of job jjj it carries, instead of the set of selected jobs alive then, only their profile: the vector x⃗\vec{x}x whose iii-th coordinate counts the selected jobs due at sj+is_j + isj​+i. Since a job's second operation is at most qmax⁡q_{\max}qmax​ long, only the coordinates 1,…,qmax⁡1, \dots, q_{\max}1,…,qmax​ can be nonzero, and each is at most mmm; the table therefore has mqmax⁡m^{q_{\max}}mqmax​ columns.

      Stepping from the previous start time to sjs_jsj​ moves the reference point by δj=sj−sj−1\delta_j = s_j - s_{j-1}δj​=sj​−sj−1​, so a profile at sj−1s_{j-1}sj−1​ becomes a profile at sjs_jsj​ shifted down by δj\delta_jδj​. The paper writes x⃗[δ]\vec{x}[\delta]x[δ] for the vectors that shift to x⃗\vec{x}x, and recursion (5) has the two branches of every such sweep: job jjj is passed over, or job jjj is selected, in which case its own coordinate qjq_jqj​ drops by one.

      Formalization Notes

      The profile is carried as a function on all i≥1i \ge 1i≥1 rather than as a vector of length qmax⁡q_{\max}qmax​. This is deliberate. The coordinates beyond qmax⁡q_{\max}qmax​ are not free: the jobs counted by the profile at sjs_jsj​ all have due dates in (sj,sj+qmax⁡](s_j, s_j + q_{\max}](sj​,sj​+qmax​], so those coordinates are zero, and a formulation that leaves them out has to say so separately — which is exactly what the printed recursion fails to do.

      The printed recursion is stated here as well, unchanged. The paper writes

      x⃗[δ]={y⃗∈{0,…,m}qmax⁡:yi=xi−δ for all i∈{δ+1,…,qmax⁡}},\vec{x}[\delta] = \{\vec{y} \in \{0,\dots,m\}^{q_{\max}} : y_i = x_{i-\delta} \text{ for all } i \in \{\delta+1, \dots, q_{\max}\}\},x[δ]={y​∈{0,…,m}qmax​:yi​=xi−δ​ for all i∈{δ+1,…,qmax​}},

      which pins y⃗\vec{y}y​ on the coordinates above δ\deltaδ and leaves the last δ\deltaδ coordinates of the shifted vector unconstrained. They are not free, for the reason just given, and leaving them so lets the recursion reach entries that no selection realizes — in both of its branches. The consequence for the algorithm is nothing at all, which is the content of the third theorem's second half: a reachable entry is still witnessed by a feasible selection, only with a profile no larger than the one recorded, and since the capacity test ∑ixi≤m\sum_i x_i \le m∑i​xi​≤m is then applied to the larger vector it can only reject more. The optimum read off at the end is exact.

      Both versions are therefore defined: the one with the missing condition supplied, whose correctness is the lemma the paper states, and the printed one, whose correctness is the weaker invariant the algorithm actually maintains. Keeping them apart is what lets both be stated.

      The printed recursion is given as an inductively defined relation — the entries it derives — rather than as a table of values, since that is what "the recursion reaches this entry" means and it needs no order of evaluation. Its coordinates are numbered from zero, so coordinate ccc counts the jobs due at sj+c+1s_j + c + 1sj​+c+1.
