      Theorem

      Let a finite family of mutually independent random variables determine a finite family of measurable bad events. For a bad event AAA, let Γ(A)\Gamma(A)Γ(A) be the other bad events sharing a variable with AAA. If there are numbers x(A)∈(0,1)x(A)\in(0,1)x(A)∈(0,1) such that

      Pr⁡[A]≤x(A)∏B∈Γ(A)(1−x(B)),\Pr[A] \le x(A)\prod_{B\in\Gamma(A)}(1-x(B)),Pr[A]≤x(A)B∈Γ(A)∏​(1−x(B)),

      then some assignment avoids every bad event. Moreover, the Moser–Tardos resampling algorithm resamples each AAA at most an expected x(A)/(1−x(A))x(A)/(1-x(A))x(A)/(1−x(A)) times, so its expected total number of resamplings is at most the sum of these bounds.
