      Theorem

      Whether a graph has a vertex cover of at most k vertices — a set touching every edge — can be decided in branchCountbranchCountbranchCount(k) times linear time, where branchCountbranchCountbranchCount is the leaf count of the search tree that branches a budget b into b−1 and b−3: there are one word RAM program and one constant c such that, at every word length, given any graph in compressed sparse row form followed by the single entry k as a word x for which c(|x|+k+1) is at most 2w2 ^ w2w, the program halts within c·branchCountbranchCountbranchCount(k)·(|x|+1) steps and writes 111 if the graph has a vertex cover of at most k vertices and 000 if it does not. branchCountbranchCountbranchCount grows as β^k for the real root β ≈ 1.4656 of x³ = x² + 1.

      The bound comes from a sharper branching rule. Branching on an edge gives 2^k; branching on a vertex with two or more uncovered edges on it gives the Fibonacci recurrence, and that is the statement Vertex cover in Fibonacci-of-k time of this same submission, with base φ ≈ 1.618. Branch on a vertex v with three distinct uncovered neighbours instead. Either v is in the cover, which costs one unit of budget, or it is not, and then all three or more of its remaining neighbours are, which costs at least three. So the leaf count obeys T(b) ≤ T(b−1) + T(b−3), and its base is the real root of x³ = x² + 1.

      The price of the sharper rule is a harder leaf. When no unmarked vertex has three distinct uncovered neighbours, every one of them has at most two, so the uncovered edges form a disjoint union of paths and cycles — and a path or a cycle with e edges is covered by ⌈e/2⌉ vertices and by no fewer. The leaf is therefore not a count of edges, as it is one rung down, but a sum of ⌈e/2⌉ over the connected components of what is left, computed by one breadth-first sweep. That the sum is exactly the cover number of a graph of maximum degree two is the one new theorem under this statement.

      This sharpens Vertex cover in Fibonacci-of-k time, which sharpens Vertex cover in explicit fixed-parameter time — all three statements of this same submission. The three are the same problem, the same machine, the same input format and the same shape of statement, with the base of the exponential lowered twice: branchCountbranchCountbranchCount(k) ≤ Nat.fibNat.fibNat.fib(k+2) for every k, with equality exactly at k ≤ 2 and strict inequality from k = 3 on (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 13, 19, 28 against 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34, 55). Neither earlier statement is withdrawn or weakened: both are proved and both remain true. And 1.4656 is not the end of the ladder either — bases below 1.3 are known, from branching rules with reduction rules in front of them, and nothing here competes with those analyses.

      56# Formalization notes
      57
      58The count is stated by its defining recurrence and not by a power. The
      59base β is the real root of *x*³ = *x*² + 1, an irrational number a
      60little above 1.4656, and a bound of the form `c * β ^ k * (x.length + 1)`
      61would have to round it somewhere. Every rounding is a small claim about
      62growth that the algorithm does not make: the search tree really does
      63have `branchCount`(*k*) leaves, exactly, and that count is what the
      64proof produces. The same reasoning put `Nat.fib` rather than φ^*k* into
      65the statement one rung down, and it is the same reasoning that writes
      66the parameter dependence into the bound at all rather than quantifying
      67it away behind "some computable *f*" — the claim is meant to be
      68falsifiable at every single *k*, and improving the base is then a change
      69of statement rather than a change of commentary. Here it forces one
      70definition onto the surface, since mathlib has no name for this
      71sequence; `Nat.fib` was mathlib's, and this is the one place where this
      72ladder has had to define something of its own.
      73
      74The three initial values are the exact leaf counts, not a convention.
      75The algorithm branches whenever it finds a branching vertex and has any
      76budget at all, and the second child's budget is the remaining budget
      77less the residual degree, truncated at zero. So a budget of 0 admits no
      78branch and the tree is one leaf; a budget of 1 branches into two
      79children both measured at budget 0, giving 2; a budget of 2 branches
      80into a child at budget 1 and a child at budget 0, giving 3. Those are
      81`branchCount 0 = 1`, `branchCount 1 = 2`, `branchCount 2 = 3`, and the
      82recurrence `branchCount (b+3) = branchCount (b+2) + branchCount b` above
      83them. Rounding the initials up to a clean power would have been a
      84weaker theorem wearing a smaller-looking bound.
      85
      86The admissible set is unchanged, character for character. An instance is
      87admissible at word length `w` when `c * (|x| + k + 1) ≤ 2 ^ w`, the same
      88inequality against `2 ^ w`, with the same reading: what has to fit into
      89a word is every number the machine holds — the entries of the graph
      90block, which are vertex numbers, offsets and the two header numbers, all
      91smaller than the block is long; the parameter, which is an entry of the
      92word, since a machine at word length `w` sees its input reduced modulo
      93`2 ^ w` and a parameter that is not the parameter it was handed is not
      94the parameter; and the bookkeeping of the search, a stack of frames and
      95a trail of marked vertices, both bounded by the block. The constant `c`
      96differs from the earlier statements' — it is bigger, and the driver has
      97two more arrays — but the *form* of the condition does not, so the
      98improved bound is claimed on exactly the same instances and not on a
      99smaller class. The condition is deliberately not coupled to the running
      100time: `branchCount k` counts leaves and is never a number the machine
      101holds, and requiring it to be a word would exclude every instance with a
      102large parameter on a small machine, which is exactly where the improved
      103base is interesting.
      104
      105The fitting condition is a condition on the admissible inputs and not a
      106hypothesis of the claim, because as a hypothesis it would be empty: a
      107graph with an edge has encodings of every length, since a block may list
      108a neighbour repeatedly, so no word length accommodates all encodings of
      109a fixed graph at once.
      110
      111The program and the constant are quantified ahead of the parameter and
      112ahead of the word length, so a single program with a single constant
      113serves every graph, every *k* and every *w* — the uniform notion of
      114fixed-parameter tractability, and a stronger statement than one program
      115per parameter or one program per word length.
      116
      117The answer is stated through mathlib's vertex cover number, so the
      118output is a function of the graph and the parameter and the surface adds
      119no definition beyond the count. The decision problem is the honest scope
      120of the claim; a program producing a cover, or a smallest one, is a
      121different theorem with a different bound. Only encodings are admitted as
      122inputs; the program may behave arbitrarily on words that encode nothing,
      123and on words too long for its word length.

      Formalization notes

      The count is stated by its defining recurrence and not by a power. The base β is the real root of x³ = x² + 1, an irrational number a little above 1.4656, and a bound of the form c∗βk∗(x.length+1)c * β ^ k * (x.length + 1)c∗βk∗(x.length+1) would have to round it somewhere. Here it forces one definition onto the surface, since mathlib has no name for this sequence; Nat.fibNat.fibNat.fib was mathlib's, and this is the one place where this ladder has had to define something of its own.

      The three initial values are the exact leaf counts, not a convention. The algorithm branches whenever it finds a branching vertex and has any budget at all, and the second child's budget is the remaining budget less the residual degree, truncated at zero. So a budget of 0 admits no branch and the tree is one leaf; a budget of 1 branches into two children both measured at budget 0, giving 2; a budget of 2 branches into a child at budget 1 and a child at budget 0, giving 3.
