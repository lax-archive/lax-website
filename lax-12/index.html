      Abstract

      This submission states the core sparsity theory of nowhere dense graph classes, largely in the development of the lecture notes Sparsity of Pilipczuk and Siebertz: nowhere dense classes are uniformly quasi-wide, the shallow minors of their members have subpolynomial edge density, an edge-density bound on shallow topological minors bounds admissibility, admissibility bounds the strong coloring number, the strong coloring number bounds the weak coloring number, and — as the composition of the last four — nowhere dense classes have subpolynomial weak coloring numbers. Weak coloring numbers control how many distinct traces vertex neighborhoods leave on a set of vertices, and the last theorem of the submission draws that consequence: nowhere dense classes have almost linear neighborhood complexity.

      The concept surface has fifteen review units. Eight definitions: graph classes on the canonical vertex types; depth-rrr minors via shallow-minor models, together with nowhere denseness; the edge density of shallow minors, both as the per-graph bound ∣E(H)∣≤d ∣V(H)∣|E(H)| \le d\,|V(H)|∣E(H)∣≤d∣V(H)∣ and as the class-level predicate ∣E(H)∣≤c m1+ε|E(H)| \le c\,m^{1+\varepsilon}∣E(H)∣≤cm1+ε; depth-rrr topological minors — an injective choice of principal vertices together with internally disjoint connecting walks of length at most 2r+12r+12r+1 — with their per-graph density bound; the generalized coloring numbers wcolr\mathrm{wcol}_rwcolr​ and scolr\mathrm{scol}_rscolr​, the minima over vertex orderings of the largest weak respectively strong rrr-reachability count, with the shared subpolynomial bound predicate mo(1)m^{o(1)}mo(1); rrr-admissibility, via families of short paths from a vertex to smaller vertices that are disjoint apart from their common start; and uniform quasi-wideness, the existence — for each radius, uniformly in the requested size — of a bounded separator after whose deletion a large vertex set contains a large distance-rrr independent subset; and neighborhood complexity, the number of distinct traces N(v)∩AN(v) \cap AN(v)∩A that the vertices of a graph leave on a vertex set AAA, with the class-level predicate ∣A∣1+o(1)|A|^{1+o(1)}∣A∣1+o(1). Seven theorems: nowhere dense classes are uniformly quasi-wide (Lemma 3.4 of Chapter 4 of the notes); nowhere dense classes have subpolynomial shallow-minor density (Theorem 3.1 of Chapter 1); a depth-rrr topological edge-density bound ddd forces admr+1≤1+6(r+1)d3\mathrm{adm}_{r+1} \le 1 + 6(r+1)d^3admr+1​≤1+6(r+1)d3 (Lemma 3.2 of Chapter 2); scolr≤1+(admr−1)r\mathrm{scol}_r \le 1 + (\mathrm{adm}_r - 1)^rscolr​≤1+(admr​−1)r (Lemma 2.5); wcolr≤1+r (scolr−1)r\mathrm{wcol}_r \le 1 + r\,(\mathrm{scol}_r - 1)^rwcolr​≤1+r(scolr​−1)r (Lemma 2.6); and the headline, that nowhere dense classes have subpolynomial weak coloring numbers, uniformly over subgraphs of members (Theorem 3.4 of Chapter 2); and that nowhere dense classes have almost linear neighborhood complexity — the radius-1 case of the theorem of Eickmeyer, Giannopoulou, Kreutzer, Kwon, Pilipczuk, Rabinovich and Siebertz, which the notes discuss but cite as a result of the literature.

      The two links of the coloring-number chain are stated separately rather than as the combined Corollary 2.7 of the notes: they have disjoint proofs, and each is citable and usable on its own. All parameters are infima of explicit sets of naturals, and all statements are phrased over the canonical finite vertex types Fin n\mathrm{Fin}\ nFin n. The admissibility bound is stated in the topological form the notes prove, over the topological grad ∇~r\tilde\nabla_r∇~r​ rather than over the ordinary grad, with the radius index shifted by one so that the hypothesis sits at depth rrr and the conclusion at radius r+1r+1r+1 — the notes' pairing of depth r−1r-1r−1 with radius rrr, without truncated natural subtraction.

      The headline theorem is not proved from scratch. It is discharged by a glue proof that assumes the four preceding theorem concepts — subpolynomial shallow-minor density, the admissibility bound, and the two coloring-number links — and performs only the arithmetic composing them, so the internal proof network of the submission is visible on the archive instead of being buried inside a single derivation. The neighborhood-complexity theorem likewise assumes that headline statement rather than importing its proof, and contributes the radius-1 trace counting, the VC-dimension bound coming from Kt,tK_{t,t}Kt,t​-freeness, the localization to a polynomially small witness set, and the exponent rescaling. The other theorem concepts are discharged by a port of the development accompanying the lecture notes: a Ramsey-theoretic extraction of distance-independent sets for quasi-wideness, a Chernoff-based densification argument for the density theorem, and the tree-counting and path-routing arguments for the coloring-number chain, each bridged to the submitted concepts. Ramsey's theorem itself is not reproved here: the quasi-wideness proof assumes the two Ramsey statements of the submission Finite Ramsey Theorems for Pairs and Tuples, so that dependency is visible on the archive as well.

      Except for the neighborhood-complexity theorem, all material is from the lecture notes Sparsity of Michał Pilipczuk and Sebastian Siebertz, taught at the University of Warsaw; the numbering above follows the 2019/20 edition of the course, whose 2017/18 predecessor carries the same statements under sequential numbering. The neighborhood-complexity bound is due to Eickmeyer, Giannopoulou, Kreutzer, Kwon, Pilipczuk, Rabinovich and Siebertz; the radius-1 derivation from the weak coloring numbers formalized here follows Corollary 6b of Dreier, Mählmann, McCarty, Pilipczuk and Toruńczyk.
