      Natural Language Statement

      Theorem

      A parameter κ′\kappa'κ′ of a problem is dominated by a parameter κ\kappaκ if κ′(x,D)≤g(κ(x,D))\kappa'(x, D) \le g(\kappa(x, D))κ′(x,D)≤g(κ(x,D)) for a function ggg and every instance xxx and threshold DDD. Then the identity on words is an fpt-reduction from the problem with κ\kappaκ to the problem with κ′\kappa'κ′ when ggg is computable, and the words with κ≤k\kappa \le kκ≤k are among those with κ′≤g(0)+⋯+g(k)\kappa' \le g(0) + \dots + g(k)κ′≤g(0)+⋯+g(k).

      Theorem. If κ′\kappa'κ′ is dominated by κ\kappaκ, then para-NP-hardness and W[t]-hardness for κ\kappaκ hold for κ′\kappa'κ′, and membership in XP and fixed-parameter tractability for κ′\kappa'κ′ hold for κ\kappaκ (ggg computable for the last two).

      Two dominations of the zoo's parameters: a summand is dominated by a sum, and on the operations family the number #p\#p#p of distinct processing times is at most pmax⁡+1p_{\max} + 1pmax​+1 (the values lie in {0,…,pmax⁡}\{0, \dots, p_{\max}\}{0,…,pmax​}).

      Theorem. These dominations hold.
