  1. thm✓Lax871432.LovaszTheorem

    Lovász's theorem

    Lovász (1967): two finite graphs are isomorphic if and only if they are homomorphism indistinguishable over all graphs, i.e. if and only if hom⁡(K,G)=hom⁡(K,H)\hom(K, G) = \hom(K, H)hom(K,G)=hom(K,H) for every graph KKK.

    It suffices to test the graphs KKK on vertex set {0,…,m−1}\{0, \dots, m-1\}{0,…,m−1}, since every finite graph is isomorphic to one of these and hom⁡(−,G)\hom(-, G)hom(−,G) is an isomorphism invariant.

    The theorem is deduced from the invertibility of the homomorphism matrix of a family of graphs. Let F1,…,FNF_1, \dots, F_NF1​,…,FN​ be pairwise non-isomorphic graphs on at most nnn vertices which represent every isomorphism class of graphs on at most nnn vertices. Then the matrix Mij=hom⁡(Fi,Fj)M_{ij} = \hom(F_i, F_j)Mij​=hom(Fi​,Fj​) is invertible over Q\mathbb{Q}Q.

  30. defLax871432.LoopGraphs

    Graphs with loops

    A loop graph is a graph in which loops are allowed: a symmetric, not necessarily irreflexive, relation on a vertex type. Simple graphs are the loopless case, and a loop graph without loops is a simple graph again. A homomorphism of loop graphs sends adjacent vertices to adjacent vertices, so a loop is sent to a loop and an edge to an edge or to a loop, and hom⁡(X,Y)\hom(X, Y)hom(X,Y) counts these maps as for simple graphs. Besides homomorphisms and isomorphisms, loop graphs carry here the full complement X^\widehat{X}X, which replaces every edge by a non-edge and every loop by a non-loop, the sub-loop-graph induced on a set of vertices, and the edge set, which for a loop graph may contain a pair vvvvvv, one for each loop.

    Loops arise from two constructions on simple graphs. The looped graph G∘G^\circG∘ is obtained from a simple graph GGG by adding a loop at every vertex; the complement then factors as G‾=G∘^\overline{G} = \widehat{G^\circ}G=G∘, which is what makes a two-step expansion of homomorphism counts into a complement possible.

    The other is a quotient. For a simple graph FFF and a set LLL of unordered pairs of vertices, the contraction quotient F⊘LF \oslash LF⊘L has as vertices the connected components of the graph on V(F)V(F)V(F) with edge set LLL, and joins two of them when some edge of E(F)∖LE(F) \setminus LE(F)∖L joins a vertex of the one to a vertex of the other. It is a graph obtained from FFF by contracting the edges of LLL whenever it is loopless; in general it is not, carrying a loop at a component for every edge of E(F)∖LE(F) \setminus LE(F)∖L with both endpoints inside it, which is why loops must be allowed here.

    Two operations on simple graphs accompany these: the spanning subgraph FsF_sFs​, which keeps all vertices of FFF and those of its edges that lie in a set sss, and the passage from a set of edges of FFF to the underlying set of unordered pairs.
